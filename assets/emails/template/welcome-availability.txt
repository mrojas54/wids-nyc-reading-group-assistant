Plain-text twin of welcome-availability.html — the multipart/alternative body.

Reproduced from the handoff README's verbatim block, with its bracket
placeholders swapped for this repo's Mustache tokens.

WRAPPING. The handoff shows this body hard-wrapped at ~68 characters. That is
a property of the OUTPUT, not of this file. Prose paragraphs are stored here
as one long logical line each and are wrapped to 68 columns by
scripts/welcome_availability._wrap_txt after substitution. Do not hand-wrap
them back: the README's pre-wrapped copy was laid out around a short
"[Member]" placeholder, and a real name ("Michelle Rojas") pushed the last
word of a line onto its own line, because line-by-line wrapping cannot
re-flow a paragraph that was already broken for a different token width.

Structural lines — section headers, the aligned Cadence/Evenings/Your hosts
block, the three bullets, the bare URLs — stay one per line and are short
enough that the wrapper never touches them. A line with no spaces is never
wrapped, which is how the URLs survive intact.

[[BEGIN:<name>]] / [[END:<name>]] lines are block markers consumed by the
composer. It drops a section here whenever the same block is toggled off in
the HTML, so the two bodies cannot drift. The markers never survive
composition — if you see one in a sent email, the body was substituted
directly instead of composed.

The court-voice header (block B) has no twin analogue by design; the twin
opens at the greeting either way.

GREETING. Both bodies read "Hey Queen," — the operator's pick of register for
handoff Open Question 5. The README's twin read "Hi [First name],"; that is
resolved in favour of the group's in-house address, so neither body carries a
first-name token. Keep the two greetings identical if either changes.

One deviation from the handoff's verbatim block: the quote attribution sits
on its own line rather than trailing the quote, so a long quote can never
push the wrap into the author's name ("— Katherine / Johnson"). The HTML
marks that attribution white-space:nowrap for the same reason.
